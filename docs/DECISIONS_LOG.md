# Decisions Log

Every important choice and assumption, with the reason. Interviewers often ask "why did you choose X?". The answers live here.

| # | Date | Decision | Why | Alternatives considered |
|---|------|----------|-----|------------------------|
| 1 | 2026-11-05 | Use the Intel microprocessor dataset (Manary & Willems 2022) as the SKU-level data | It is real semiconductor demand, and it contains the company's own forecast next to actual orders. That lets us measure forecast accuracy, bias and Forecast Value Add the way a planning team would. | UCI Online Retail (not semiconductor), M5 Walmart (retail), a fully synthetic dataset (not real) |
| 2 | 2026-11-05 | Add FRED / Census M3 market signals | Semiconductor planners watch end-market indicators; these are free and official | WSTS/SIA data (press-release only, harder to automate) |
| 3 | 2026-11-05 | Primary accuracy metric = WMAPE (accuracy = 1 − WMAPE) | It weights errors by volume and does not blow up on low or zero weeks like MAPE does; it is the common industry KPI | MAPE, RMSE, MASE (reported as secondary) |
| 4 | 2026-11-05 | Test bias significance (HAC t-test + Wilcoxon), not just bias size | It separates systematic bias that needs action from random noise that is tolerable | Tracking signal only |
| 5 | 2026-11-05 | Do not reuse the old resume numbers | They came from a different dataset; only measured results get reported | — |
| 6 | 2026-11-08 | Weeks missing *inside* a SKU-DC's life are filled with **orders = 0** (flag `recorded = False`); Intel's forecast for those weeks stays blank (NaN) | Orders are never 0 in the file, but forecasts sometimes are, so rows exist only when orders were placed. Ignoring the gaps would overstate demand and hide intermittency. **Assumption:** not confirmed by public sources. Day 2 runs a sensitivity check (gaps as zero vs. excluded) | Leave gaps out (overstates demand); forward-fill (invents demand) |
| 7 | 2026-11-08 | Treat `Forecasted Demand` as Intel's forecast **recorded for week t** (same-week), with lag/horizon unknown | Its correlation with orders peaks at offset 0; it is far smaller than multi-week order totals; the paper abstract describes "weekly forecasted demand". We do not claim a specific lag | Assume a cumulative or multi-week forecast (contradicted by the data) |
| 8 | 2026-11-08 | Score Intel's forecast **per DC**, not only in total | Forecast ÷ orders ranges from 1.06× (BETA) to 4.74× (EPSILON). A single total hides where the problem is. The paper reports DC heterogeneity too | One portfolio-level KPI |
| 9 | 2026-11-08 | Use FRED series as **market context only**, not as model features | The Intel weeks have no calendar dates, so any alignment would be a guess, which risks learning fake relationships | Guess a start date and align (rejected: unverifiable) |
| 10 | 2026-11-08 | Assume **higher ASP group = higher price** | Newer generations sit in higher groups (Spearman ρ = 0.28, p = 0.010). Weak evidence: Day 5 ABC results will be checked under the reverse ordering | Treat ASP group as unordered |
| 11 | 2026-11-08 | Never quote absolute unit volumes or dollar values as real Intel figures | A search snippet suggests quantities were scaled to anonymise them (unconfirmed). Ratios, WMAPE and bias % are scale-free and safe to report | — |
| 12 | 2026-11-08 | Flag SKUs already selling in week 1 (`launched_before_data`); build life-cycle curves only from SKUs launched inside the window | All Gen 1–2 SKUs were on the market before week 1, so their "weeks since launch" is not a true launch age | Treat week 1 as launch for all SKUs (biases life-cycle features) |
